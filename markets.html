Who I work with

Wherever you export from, Russia works the same way once you land.

The retail chains, marketplaces, margins and certificates don't change with your passport. What changes is how you get paid, what Russian shoppers already think of your country's products, and who you'll compete with. Below are the questions I hear most, by region.

Turkey → Russia

You already export. Russia is close, big and price-sensitive.

It also works very differently from EU retail. I help Turkish brands pick a channel, find a distributor and get the price math right before the first order.

  • Should we start with marketplaces or a distributor?
  • What margin will Russian retail chains expect?
  • Which certificates do we need for our category?
  • How do we get paid safely?
South Korea → Russia

The hard part is choosing who represents your brand.

I help Korean brands find, check and manage the right partner, so you keep control of pricing and positioning instead of handing both to a distributor.

  • How do we stop our price from collapsing on marketplaces?
  • Is an exclusive distributor a good idea for us?
  • What does a Russian distributor expect in the first year?
India → Russia

Trade between the two is growing. Retail knowledge isn't.

Many Indian exporters know the B2B route well and the Russian retail shelf much less. I help you see what the shelf and the marketplace need from your brand.

  • Can our price compete with brands already on the shelf?
  • Which marketplace fits our category?
  • What labelling do we need in Russian?
China → Russia

Every Chinese brand is here. Standing out takes more than price.

I know how distributors compare you with the ten other brands in their price list.

  • How do we get a national distributor to prioritise us?
  • What goes wrong when we sell to several distributors at once?
  • How do we build a brand, not just a SKU on Wildberries?
UAE & Middle East → Russia

Trading hubs, re-exporters and brand owners.

If you own the brand, you need someone who protects it, not just moves boxes. I help you set up a direct, clean route to Russian retail.

  • Should we sell through a trader or appoint a distributor?
  • How do we keep control of the brand across several importers?
Southeast Asia → Russia

Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia and beyond.

Food, cosmetics, home goods and electronics components are finding their way to Russia. I help you figure out which channel values your product and how to reach it.

  • Is our category crowded or open?
  • What do we need before a buyer takes a meeting?
Japan → Russia

Careful decisions, long partnerships.

Japanese companies usually want to understand everything before moving. I give you the written, detailed picture your HQ needs to approve a step, and a partner check that holds up.

  • Which partner can we trust for ten years, not one?
  • What are the compliance limits for our category?
Latin America → Russia

Far away, but not as far as it looks.

Brands from Brazil, Argentina, Chile and Mexico ask me about logistics, payment and whether their brand story will work for Russian shoppers.

  • Is the distance going to kill our margin?
  • Who buys our category in Russia today?
Everywhere else

Central Asia, the Caucasus, Africa, Europe and elsewhere

If your country and product allow lawful trade with Russia, the process is the same: a snapshot, a decision, the right partner, and someone who watches your interests after the contract is signed. If sanctions or export rules in your country limit what you can do, we'll check that first. Some projects end right there, and that's the correct outcome.
